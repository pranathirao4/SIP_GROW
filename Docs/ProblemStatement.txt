## 2. Business problem

You are a Product Manager on the Growth Team at Groww.

Our strategic goal is:

Increase the percentage of first-time mutual fund investors who still have an active SIP 12 months after starting their first SIP.

An active SIP means at least one successful instalment in the last 30 days.

Starting a SIP is easy, but users may subsequently reduce, pause, cancel, or discontinue their investments.

We have not been given the underlying user problem. Our task is to discover it through evidence-based research.

The solution must not:
- Recommend specific mutual funds or stocks.
- Use fear-based, FOMO-based, or urgency-based nudges.
- Treat assumptions as validated user problems.
- Recommend solutions before establishing the problem.

## 3. Primary objective of the agent

Build an AI discovery system that collects, organizes, filters, analyzes, and synthesizes public conversations about SIP investing in India.

It must help answer:

1. Why do people start their first SIP, and what do they expect?
2. What changes in their behaviour during the months after starting?
3. What leads investors to reduce, pause, cancel, or discontinue SIPs?
4. What do investors check, and how frequently, after starting?
5. What confuses or worries them inside investing apps?
6. What do they do outside the app when they feel uncertain?
7. What differentiates investors who continue from those who discontinue?
8. How do these patterns vary by income stability, age, source of money, investing experience, and social influence?
9. What unmet needs emerge consistently?
10. Which potential product opportunities could plausibly improve 12-month SIP retention?

Distinguish between evidence directly observed in conversations, hypotheses inferred from that evidence, and questions that require further research.

## 6. Build the data processing pipeline

Implement the following stages in order.

### Stage A: Raw data ingestion

- Import Apify datasets and CSV/JSON files.
- Preserve the original source text and URL.
- Assign a unique record ID.
- Record the source, collection date, and available metadata.
- Retain the original dataset for auditing.

### Stage B: Data cleaning

- Remove empty and malformed records.
- Normalize text without changing its meaning.
- Detect exact duplicates.
- Identify near-duplicates and cross-posts where feasible.
- Preserve links between comments and parent discussions.
- Do not incorrectly treat every comment within a thread as an independent conversation.
- Keep original text immutable.

### Stage C: Relevance filtering

Classify records into:

1. Directly relevant to starting, continuing, reducing, pausing, cancelling, or missing SIP instalments.
2. Indirectly relevant to first-time investor behaviour, uncertainty, expectations, or decision-making.
3. Relevant to app usability, mandates, payment failures, portfolio visibility, or investor education.
4. General investing content with insufficient evidence of a SIP-retention connection.
5. Irrelevant or unusable content.

Allow records to have multiple relevant categories where appropriate.

For each record, produce:
- Relevance category.
- Relevance score.
- Short explanation.
- Evidence supporting the classification.
- Confidence level.
- Human review flag when uncertain.

Use a configurable threshold and allow me to inspect borderline records before excluding them.

Do not equate an app complaint with a retention problem unless the evidence supports the connection.

### Stage D: AI classification

For every relevant record, classify:

- Investor stage: considering, first-time, continuing, paused, cancelled, or unknown.
- Reported behaviour: started, continued, increased, reduced, paused, cancelled, failed instalment, or unknown.
- Stated motivation for starting.
- Expectations before starting.
- Trigger or context.
- Explicitly stated reason for the behaviour.
- Reported pain point.
- Underlying user need, if supported.
- Emotional signal, if explicitly evident.
- App-specific issue versus broader financial problem.
- External actions taken to resolve uncertainty.
- Source of influence, if explicitly mentioned.
- Income stability, age, and source of money only when supported by evidence.
- Evidence type: direct, indirect, or speculative.
- Confidence and rationale.
- Exact supporting quotation.
- Source URL and record ID.

Do not invent demographic attributes or infer that someone is a first-time investor without evidence.

### Stage E: Theme discovery and clustering

Create an initial taxonomy that can evolve with the evidence.

Potential starting themes include:
- Affordability and cash-flow constraints.
- Competing financial priorities.
- Expectations versus actual experience.
- Confusion about investment performance.
- Market volatility and uncertainty.
- Low perceived progress.
- Lack of financial understanding.
- Missed instalments and forgetfulness.
- Mandate and payment failures.
- App usability and information clarity.
- Social influence and external advice.
- Trust and customer support.
- Changes in personal circumstances.

These are starting hypotheses, not predetermined conclusions.

Implement AI-assisted clustering or semantic grouping if practical. Use deterministic rules for straightforward labels and AI for ambiguous text and deeper interpretation.

Allow multiple themes per record.

For every cluster, generate:
- Theme name.
- Operational definition.
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria.
- Number of matching records.
- Number of unique posts and conversations.
- Number of independent sources represented.
- Relevant investor stages.
- Verbatim supporting quotes.
- Source links.
- Counterexamples.
- Confidence.
- Potential retention connection.
- Open research questions.

Detect emerging themes without forcing all records into the existing taxonomy.

Move beyond sentiment analysis and summaries.

Identify:
- Recurring user problems.
- Triggers that precede reported behaviour changes.
- Unmet needs.
- Differences between stated motivations and subsequent experiences.
- Workarounds users employ outside the app.
- Differences between continuing and discontinuing investors.
- Conflicting evidence and alternative explanations.
- Potentially addressable product opportunities.

For each proposed insight, provide:

2. User problem hypothesis.
3. Evidence count and denominator.
4. Unique conversations and sources.
5. Supporting verbatim quotations.
6. Counterevidence.
7. Relevant investor segments.
8. Strength of the connection to SIP continuation.
9. Confidence and limitations.
10. What remains unknown.
11. Recommended survey or interview question.

Never generate a finding merely because it sounds plausible.
